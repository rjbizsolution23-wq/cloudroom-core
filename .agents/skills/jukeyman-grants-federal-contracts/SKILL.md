## 1. GRANTS
Research: Grants.gov, agency portals (NSF Research.gov, NIH eRA, DOE PAMS/FedConnect), SBIR.gov
topics, state grants, foundations (Candid/Foundation Directory), corporate giving. Evaluate
eligibility, cost share/match, deadline, fit, capacity; recommend go/no-go.
Federal rules: 2 CFR 200 Uniform Guidance (2024 revisions — verify current thresholds, e.g.,
de minimis indirect rate, equipment threshold, single-audit threshold); negotiated indirect
rate if any; Grants.gov/SAM registrations take weeks — start early.

Proposal template (adapt to the NOFO's required structure):
- Cover/abstract: applicant, title, funder, opportunity number, amount, period.
- Executive summary: need, goals, approach, outcomes, budget, capacity.
- Statement of need: data-driven, cited, target population, alignment with funder priorities.
- Project description: goals → SMART objectives; methods; timeline/milestones; staffing;
  partners; evidence base/innovation.
- Evaluation plan: process + outcome measures, data collection and analysis, logic model.
- Sustainability: post-award funding, capacity, scalability.
- Organizational capacity: history, governance, financial health, relevant experience, bios,
  facilities, letters of support.
- Budget table (personnel, fringe, travel, equipment, supplies, contractual, other direct,
  indirect, total) + budget narrative justifying each line under 2 CFR 200 cost principles.
- Appendices as required (e.g., 501(c)(3) letter, org chart, CVs, letters, financials).
Writing standards: answer every evaluation criterion explicitly, use the funder's terms, no
unsupported claims, comply exactly with formatting rules.

## 3. FEDERAL CONTRACTS
Phase 1 — Registration: SAM.gov (free — warn about paid "registration" scams; UEI is assigned
in SAM; CAGE code; renew every 365 days; Reps & Certs, FAR 52.204-8). NAICS codes and SBA size
standards. Certifications via SBA (MySBA Certifications): small business, WOSB/EDWOSB, VOSB/SDVOSB,
HUBZone, 8(a) (new disadvantage rules). Past performance: CPARS (PPIRS was retired into CPARS);
capability statement.
Phase 2 — Opportunities: SAM.gov Contract Opportunities, agency forecasts, GSA MAS, set-asides,
subcontracting with primes (SBA SubNet), sources-sought/RFI responses. Bid/no-bid on fit,
evaluation criteria, competition, capacity, win probability.
Phase 3 — Proposal (follow Section L/M or the RFQ instructions exactly):
- Vol I Technical: executive summary; understanding of requirements; technical approach
  mapped to each requirement; WBS, schedule, QA, risk; management approach, key personnel,
  staffing, subcontracting, communication.
- Past performance: 3–5 relevant projects (customer, value, period, scope, results, POC).
- Vol II Price/Cost: by CLIN/year (labor categories/hours/rates, materials, travel, ODCs,
  indirect rates, fee); narrative; comply with the pricing instructions.
- Vol III Compliance: reps/certs, subcontracting plan if required, Buy American/trade
  agreements, OCI disclosures, security requirements (e.g., CMMC for DoD where applicable).
Phase 4 — Post-award: kickoff with CO/COR; deliverables and reporting; clause compliance
(including the EO 14398 clause and flow-downs); invoicing; CPARS; mods and follow-on work.
FCA warning: certifications in SAM, proposals, and invoices are legal statements — accuracy matters.

## 5. INCLUSION & EQUAL OPPORTUNITY PROGRAMS (formerly the "DEI strategy" deliverable)
Goal: fair, merit-based, legally defensible practices that widen opportunity for everyone.
Mandatory first step: identify whether the client is a federal contractor/subcontractor or
grant recipient, and its states/countries. Recommend employment counsel review before launch.
Generally lower-risk practices (confirm with counsel):
- Structured interviews, consistent rubrics, broad outreach that doesn't exclude anyone.
- Mentoring, sponsorship, training, and ERGs open to all employees.
- Pay-equity audits conducted through counsel (privilege), with remediation.
- Accessibility (ADA), accommodations, anti-harassment and anti-retaliation policies.
- Engagement surveys; manager training on fair evaluation; transparent promotion criteria.
- Supplier programs open to all small businesses; use the lawful federal small-business
  categories.
High-risk — flag and don't draft without counsel: race- or sex-based hiring/promotion targets or
quotas; programs, fellowships, or ERGs restricted by race/sex; demographic-based supplier
spend targets; tying pay to demographic outcomes. For federal contractors, these can trigger
contract termination, debarment, or FCA liability.
Governance: an inclusion/opportunity council, metrics on process fairness (time-to-hire,
promotion-rate consistency, pay-equity results), periodic legal review. Non-U.S. clients: follow
local law (e.g., UK Equality Act, EU pay transparency directive).

## 6. FRANCHISE DEVELOPMENT
Feasibility: proven unit economics, documented systems, brand/IP, capital, management. Model:
single/multi-unit, area development, master; territory; fees (initial, royalty, brand fund);
investment ranges (Item 7). Operations manual, training, field support, tech stack, marketing.
Legal: FTC Franchise Rule (16 CFR 436): FDD Items 1–23 delivered at least 14 calendar days before
signing or payment; registration states (commonly 14 — verify list) and filing/notice states;
trademark registration; FTC 2024 policy statements on undisclosed fees and non-disparagement
clauses. Financial performance: any earnings or ROI claims to prospects must be in Item 19 with
a reasonable basis — franchisee projections stay internal or go into a compliant Item 19.
Recruitment: candidate profile, lead sources, discovery day, validation, brokers (check state
broker registration rules). Roadmap: foundation → pilot (3–5 units) → growth → maturity.

## 7. STRATEGIC COMMUNICATIONS & COPYWRITING
Comms plan: situation analysis, stakeholders, SWOT, objectives (baselined and measurable), key
messages and proof points, channels (owned/earned/paid), crisis plan (severity tiers, team,
holding statements, spokesperson training), measurement cadence.
Copy frameworks: homepage (hero headline/subhead/CTA, value props, social proof, features, CTA),
email (subject, preview, hook, value, proof, single CTA, P.S.), sales landing page
(problem → solution → how it works → benefits → proof → offer → FAQ → CTA), LinkedIn post,
Instagram caption, X thread.
Truth rules: claims and numbers must be substantiated ("30% more revenue" needs evidence);
real testimonials with consent (FTC fake reviews rule, 16 CFR 465); typical-results disclosures;
no fake scarcity; government-contract marketing can't imply agency endorsement.
